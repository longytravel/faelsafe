## What we'd actually focus on

Once we take out everything BigChange already does, four things are left. All four sit
outside your CRM's remit, which is exactly why they've been missed.

### 1. The standards and bulletin watch — your idea, and the best one in the document

You wrote that when new standards or technical bulletins are published, staying on top
of them and working out which customers are affected is regularly missed, that it's
required for your accreditations, and that service cycles often tighten when standards
are published — which increases your revenue, unless it goes missed.

We went looking for something that already does this. There isn't one. BSI sell a
change-monitoring product, but it's built for medical device manufacturers. Barbour
sell standards monitoring, but it's aimed at health-and-safety managers and it stops at
telling you what changed. **Nothing tells a contractor which of their own clients a
change affects, and prompts them to go and advise them.** That's the part that makes
you money, and it's the part nobody sells.

What we'd build watches the published sources, spots a change, works out which of your
clients have that type of system, and puts a prompt in front of the right person:
*"BS 5839-1 has been revised — these eleven clients have that system — here's the
advisory letter, do you want to send it?"*

One honest limit: British Standards are copyright and sold by BSI, so it can tell you
a standard has changed and point you to the official summary, but it can't reproduce
the text. Neither can anyone else, legally.

### 2. Your website

You said you loved the draft. It's largely built. Getting it live is the quickest
visible win available, and it fixes some genuinely serious problems — your current site
has no headline tag or description on a single page, nothing a search engine can read
about your business, and analytics that stopped recording in **July 2023**. That last
one matters more than it sounds: it means nobody can currently prove where a single
enquiry comes from.

### 3. Private-sector leads, running automatically

You said the offering leaned too public-sector. Fair. The two strongest sources we
verified are the ones we under-sold:

**Care homes.** As you say, most are private companies with their own budgets. The
Care Quality Commission rates every one of them on "Safe" — which explicitly covers
fire safety, maintenance checks and equipment records — and publishes the list free
every month. A home marked down on Safe has an inspector coming back and a problem to
fix.

**Enforcement notices.** You were right that some of these go nowhere — landlords who
won't spend, tenants who aren't the landlord, the cheap sticky-plaster fix, time
wasters in Bradford and Dewsbury. But you also made the argument better than we did:
if 30% come through, they're leads that would never have existed, and if it's
automated there's no cost per lead.

For the record, the numbers are real. We checked them against the register rather than
estimating: **786 buildings in West Yorkshire are under a live fire safety enforcement
notice right now.** 549 were served in the last twelve months. **604 of them are on
high-rise flats, warehouses, care homes, hotels, schools and hospitals** — your
customer base, almost exactly. Each one names the premises and the responsible company.

### 4. Live tender tracking

You described using several tender portals that don't talk to each other, with live
tenders changing daily, questions and answers being published, requirements shifting,
and documents you've already completed needing to change. Finding tenders isn't your
problem — you've solved that. Keeping track of live ones across portals is. That's a
real piece of work and, as far as we can tell, nobody has built it for a firm your size.

### What we'd park, and say so plainly

**Live alarm signals in a portal.** You suggested pulling your CSL signals through so
clients could see their own sites. We looked: CSL don't publish a way for a third party
to get at that data — it goes to the monitoring station. Unless you can get CSL or DSOC
to open a route for you, we can't build it, and we won't pretend otherwise.

**Constructionline accreditation status.** No automated feed exists, so any display of
it would be typed in by hand and would go stale.

**The AI check of an installation from video.** This was the most original idea in your
document and we'd love to attempt it. It's also genuinely unproven. It belongs after
we've earned the right, not in a first invoice.

## How it would work

Short cycles, working software, no long documents.

**Week 1 — Switch on the measurement.** We fix your analytics and get the new website
live. Nothing else can be honestly measured until this is done, because right now you
cannot see where an enquiry comes from.

**Weeks 2–4 — Build one tool.** We'd recommend the standards watch. You get something
real to use, not a specification.

**Week 5 — You decide.** Keep it and we carry on, or stop. No notice period, no
minimum term.

After that, one new tool roughly every three to four weeks, in whatever order you
think is worth most.

## What the returns should look like, and how we'd prove them

We'd rather agree the measurements before we build than argue about them afterwards.
The honest starting position is that **most of these cannot be measured today**,
because the analytics have been dead since 2023. Fixing that is step one for exactly
this reason.

| What we build | What we measure | Baseline | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Website + analytics | Enquiries per month, and the source of each | Currently unknown — no data since July 2023. We set the baseline in week one. | Any attributable enquiry is upside, because today you cannot attribute one at all |
| Standards watch | Standards changes acted on; clients advised; service intervals increased as a result | How many did you act on last year, systematically? Our guess is close to none | Recurring revenue rises on existing contracts, with no new customer needed |
